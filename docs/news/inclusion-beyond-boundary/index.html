Inclusion · Community guide

Inclusion beyond the boundary

Cricket can start a conversation about accessibility. Lasting inclusion comes from listening, practical changes and shared participation.

A shared activity opens a conversation

Blind cricket gives a group a reason to practise listening, explaining and working together. Schools, community organisations and workplace teams can explore the game as part of a broader conversation about participation.

An experience is not someone else’s life

Trying an adapted game does not tell you everything about being blind or having low vision. Avoid treating an activity as a simulation of disability. The most useful learning includes listening to people’s own perspectives and respecting individual preferences.

Notice the practical barriers

Inclusion often begins with straightforward questions: Is the meeting point clear? Can information be read or heard in a useful format? Are instructions understandable? Can someone ask for support without being singled out?

Take the learning with you

Better communication and accessible planning matter beyond sport. Use what your group learns to review everyday meetings, activities and spaces. Small practical changes can help more people take part.

Explore a community experience

Ask Blind Cricket ACT about current blind cricket experiences, accessibility or inclusion workshops and team-building opportunities. Availability, format, venue and costs need to be confirmed with the club.
