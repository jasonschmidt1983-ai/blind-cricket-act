A game shaped by sound

An audible ball, clear calls and a shared sense of the field. Discover the essentials that make blind cricket a team game.

Listen for the ball

Blind cricket uses a ball that makes sound. That sound gives players information as they bat, bowl and field. Learning to listen for it is part of discovering the game, alongside the familiar skills of cricket.

Communication is a cricket skill

Clear calls help people understand what is happening around them. Players work together to share information, respond to the ball and make decisions. Teamwork is something you practise, not something you need to master before you arrive.

Adapted play, familiar excitement

Rules are adapted for players with different levels of vision. Formats and competition requirements can vary, so ask your session leader to explain the version you are playing. You can still enjoy the challenge of a good delivery, a well-timed shot and contributing to a team.

Try it for yourself

You do not need previous cricket experience to ask about getting started. Confirm an introductory session, what to bring and any access arrangements through Blind Cricket ACT’s official contact channel.
